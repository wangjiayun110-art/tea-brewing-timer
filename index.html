Tea Brewing Timer

A tea brewing timer does one job: pick your tea, get the temperature, the leaf amount and the steeping time, then let the timer watch the clock, so you can watch the leaves.

Most bad cups are not bad tea — and that is what a tea brewing timer is for. They are good leaves steeped in water that was too hot, or left in the water too long. This timer fixes the second half of that problem, and the numbers below fix the first.

Steeping times at a glance

These are starting points for a 250 ml mug. Your leaves, your water and your taste will move them a little, and that is normal. If a cup is too light, steep longer next time. If it is bitter, use cooler water or fewer leaves, not a longer steep.

  • Green tea: 80°C / 175°F, about 2 g, 2 minutes.
  • White tea: 85°C / 185°F, about 2 g, 3 minutes.
  • Yellow tea: 80°C / 175°F, about 2 g, 2½ minutes.
  • Oolong tea: 95°C / 200°F, about 3 g, 3 minutes.
  • Black tea: 95°C / 200°F, about 2–3 g, 3 minutes.
  • Pu-erh tea: 100°C / 212°F, about 3 g, 2 minutes. Rinse the leaves once with hot water first, and pour that rinse away.

Why timing matters more than technique

A tea brewing timer matters because tea keeps extracting as long as the leaves sit in water. A cup that tasted perfect at two minutes can turn harsh at ten. That is why the simplest habit in Chinese tea is also the most useful one: pour the tea out when the timer ends, and keep the leaves separate until the next infusion. Most Chinese teas can be steeped several times, and the second and third infusions are often the most interesting, because the leaves have fully opened. Add about 30 seconds for each later steep in a mug, or about 10 seconds in a small gongfu pot.

Questions beginners ask

How long should I steep green tea?

About 2 minutes in water around 80°C. Boiling water makes green tea bitter and flat, so let the kettle cool first.

Can I re-steep the same leaves?

Yes. Good loose-leaf tea is usually good for two to four infusions. Each later steep needs a little more time. When the flavour turns thin and flat, the leaves are done.

How much tea should I use?

About 2 grams per 250 ml mug — roughly one generous teaspoon. In a small gongfu pot, use more leaves and much shorter steeps: about 5 grams per 100 ml, starting at 20 to 45 seconds.

Do I need a special timer?

No. Any timer works. The point of this page is the numbers next to it: the right temperature and the right time for each kind of tea.
